The Journey

Golf's biggest stage, an hour from the fair.

Mission Hills is the largest golf club on earth — a Guinness World Record twelve championship courses and 216 holes across Shenzhen and Dongguan, signed by Jack Nicklaus, Greg Norman, Sir Nick Faldo, Ernie Els and Annika Sörenstam. Add the Challenge Tour's home at Foshan and an optional round beneath the red Danxia cliffs of Lion Lake, and the Greater Bay becomes a golf destination in its own right — not just the place your meetings happen to be.

At a Glance

  • Two rounds at Mission Hills — the world's largest golf club, host of the World Cup of Golf
  • Three nights in the resort's own five-star hotel, a buggy ride from the first tee
  • A round at Foshan Golf Club, private members' home of the European Challenge Tour's Foshan Open
  • Optional fourth round at Lion Lake, the only course in the world on a UNESCO-listed Danxia landform
  • A Guangzhou finale — Cantonese dining on the Pearl River, old Canton by evening
  • Private car and driver throughout, from any hotel, fairground or the Hong Kong border

Every element is a starting point — shaped to your dates, your party and your pace.

The Golf

Three rounds. Three different worlds.

Mission Hills — Two Rounds

Choose any two of the twelve. Most guests open on the World Cup Course, Jack Nicklaus's flagship and stage of the 1995 World Cup of Golf, then take on Greg Norman's — widely rated the sternest test on the property. Faldo, Els, Sörenstam and Ozaki are all a buggy ride away if you'd rather mix it differently.

Foshan Golf Club — One Round

A private members' club and home of the Foshan Open on the European Challenge Tour — a championship par-72 reshaped by Tony Cashmore, kept in tournament condition year-round and among the first courses in China to earn GEO sustainability certification. Less than an hour from central Guangzhou, with guest play arranged through us.

Optional Add-On

Lion Lake, Qingyuan

Swap a round — or stay for a fourth. Rick Jacobson's 7,242-yard Moon Course at Lion Lake — China Top 10, Asia Top 100 — runs beneath the red sandstone towers of a UNESCO-listed Danxia landscape, the only course in the world built on one, about an hour north of Guangzhou.
